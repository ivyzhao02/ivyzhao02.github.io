Context and role

Helping messages reach players.

The role sat between audience-facing communication and the detailed work required to publish it well.

I wrote, edited, published, and adapted Warframe messages across web, social, and in-product channels. The content supported announcements, events, collaborations, and live-service updates, each with different timing, assets, review needs, and audience context.

Writing and production

From interview questions to gameplay capture.

My contribution varied by deliverable. I developed developer interview questions for Warmframe, produced and revised player-facing copy, captured and edited short-form gameplay footage, and prepared visual materials for different platforms and campaign needs.

The common thread was audience judgment: deciding what a player needed to know, keeping the tone appropriate to the channel, and making sure the supporting details were ready before publication.

Events and community

Supporting TennoCon 2025.

I contributed to content production around TennoCon 2025 and supported existing event plans during a busy live-service publishing period. I also helped moderate community discussions involving sensitive subject matter, where careful wording, judgment, and escalation mattered.

This work reinforced how closely publishing, events, and community trust are connected. A message has to be accurate and timely, but it also has to make sense to the people receiving it.

Collaboration

Schedules, approvals, revisions, and release timing.

I coordinated across marketing, design, development, and global teams to help deliverables move through editorial schedules, asset requests, review, revision, approval, and publication.

I did not own media strategy or press relationships. My contribution was the practical communications and delivery work around the message: writing, editing, preparing assets, tracking dependencies, and following up with the people needed to move it forward.

Team visibility

A focused tracker alongside Jira.

I proposed and built a shared Excel tracker to complement Jira for a remote team. It made individual assignments, edit readiness, and approval status easier to see at a glance.

The tool addressed a specific visibility gap without trying to replace the team’s primary project-management platform. It was adopted into the team’s ongoing way of working.
